01 / INTENT

One shared power supply.

This project makes PC power planning visible. Choose real AMD processors, matching motherboards, graphics cards, storage and fans. Their combined planning load competes for the same 800–1200 W power supply.

The component selection and running summary are inspired by Micro Center’s PC Builder. The focus here is understanding power.

02 / PROCESS

Specs → objects → graphics.

Component objects store each part’s specification and quantity. PCBuild combines the loads, Motherboard checks the CPU socket, and PowerSupply tracks available capacity. PowerFlow draws the computer and its animated connections using p5.js.

Manufacturer links sit beside the controls. Published ratings and project assumptions are labeled separately. Motherboard power is included in the platform allowance.

03 / EMERGING BEHAVIOR

Small choices. A collective limit.

A part can fit within the power budget on its own, while its combination with other parts exceeds the supply. The warning emerges from the combined load and shared capacity.

Try the demanding build. Compare 800 W with 1200 W, then replace the RTX 5090 with the RTX 5070. The total, remaining watts and status change together.
